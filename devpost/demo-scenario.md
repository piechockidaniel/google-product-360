# Google Product 360 — Hackathon Demo Scenario

## Purpose

Show one complete operator story in four to five minutes:

> A product delivered through a feed is failing. Product 360 explains the problem, offers a safe action, runs it independently of the browser, and preserves the complete audit trail.

The public demo uses synthetic data. Say this explicitly; do not present the results as live Merchant Center data.

## Before recording

- Use a clean browser profile or an incognito window.
- Open `https://gmc.connect-the-dots.biz/`.
- Keep the browser at a readable desktop resolution.
- Do not show terminals, server credentials, service-account JSON, or private accounts.
- Use English or Polish consistently throughout the recording.
- If the public demo has been reset, create the administrator through the setup wizard.
- Confirm that the `DEMO` or synthetic-data label is visible before starting the story.

## Story and timing

### 0:00–0:25 — The problem

Open on the dashboard and say:

> “Merchant Center product information is spread across several Google surfaces. For a small ecommerce business, the important question is not only whether a product failed, but why it failed and what can safely be done next.”

Point out the dashboard health state, product totals, and recommendation or issue summary.

### 0:25–0:55 — The unified view

Open **Products**.

Show the product grid columns:

- thumbnail,
- product ID,
- title,
- supplier or brand,
- feed,
- price,
- availability,
- status,
- issue summary,
- recommendation or action state.

Apply the **Fix** recommendation filter, or use the first row that visibly offers a Quick Fix action.

Say:

> “Instead of opening several separate interfaces, the operator can start with the product and see the current state, issue severity, and the next available action in one place.”

### 0:55–1:45 — Understand before acting

Open the affected product detail pane.

Show:

- the product identity and current status,
- the issue source and issue code,
- affected attributes or destinations,
- the proposed remediation,
- supporting evidence from comparable products,
- the expected consequence,
- the distinction between predictable and review-required issues.

Say:

> “Product 360 does not turn every problem into an automatic fix. A Quick Fix is available only when the proposed value and outcome are sufficiently clear. Complex or uncertain issues remain user-driven and transparent.”

If the product has no Quick Fix, return to Products and select a row with Quick Fix available. Do not force an unsupported action.

### 1:45–2:35 — Approve a predictable fix

Click **Quick Fix**.

In the confirmation dialog, pause on:

- what will change,
- why the recommendation was made,
- what the operation affects,
- the approval responsibility.

Confirm the action.

Say:

> “The correction is not silently applied. The operator explicitly approves it, and the action becomes a durable background operation.”

Show the processing state and loader. If the result arrives quickly, show the completed state. If the UI reports that Google-side processing is still pending, say:

> “The browser is not the owner of this operation. If Google needs more time, Product 360 keeps the operation pending and reconciles the durable state later.”

### 2:35–3:15 — Prove the audit trail

Open **Activity**.

Open the operation trace and show the ordered events:

- request or approval,
- operation started,
- proposed or submitted change,
- provider result,
- final product state.

Say:

> “Every automatic, batch, or user-approved action leaves a read-only trace. This makes the result explainable and useful for support or bug investigation.”

### 3:15–3:50 — Confirm the result

Return to **Products** or the product detail pane.

Refresh if needed and show the updated status, issue count, or action state.

Say:

> “The final screen is reconstructed from the stored application state, not from the previous browser session. Closing the window would not cancel the operation.”

### 3:50–4:20 — Show the broader direction

Open the dashboard or **Market** page briefly.

Point out the recommendation categories and explain:

> “The same foundation can later combine performance, price benchmarks, best sellers, competitor visibility, and Search Console signals. Those signals can feed reusable patterns and workflows, while the audit boundary remains the same.”

Stop here unless there is time for the optional extension.

## Optional extension: Simulate a day

If the video can run longer, use the dashboard’s **Simulate a day** action.

Show:

- new and resolved issues,
- status changes,
- recommendation changes,
- activity generated by enabled demo workflows,
- the “since your last login” section.

Use this sentence:

> “The demo can simulate a later day so the operator can see how changes accumulate and how the application answers the question: what changed since my last login?”

## Closing sentence

Finish with:

> “Google Product 360 is not trying to replace Merchant Center. It creates one explainable, auditable workspace for deciding what to do with every product.”

## Recording success criteria

- The demo visibly identifies synthetic or Demo mode.
- The product grid is shown before the detail pane.
- A real Demo Quick Fix is approved only after its evidence and consequence are shown.
- The operation reaches a terminal result or is clearly shown as provider-pending.
- The read-only activity trace is visible.
- The product state is checked again after the operation.
- No credentials, private data, or unsupported live-Google claims appear in the recording.

## If something goes wrong

- If setup is required, create the administrator and continue; do not use a pre-recorded credential.
- If a selected product has no Quick Fix, choose another row with a predictable issue.
- If an operation remains pending, keep the pending state and show Activity; do not claim success.
- If the public demo is unavailable, use the local demo documented in `docs/demo.md` and keep the synthetic-data disclosure.
- If live API configuration is discussed, describe it as the next integration boundary rather than as part of the synthetic proof.
